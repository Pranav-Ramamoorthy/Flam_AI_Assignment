## 2. Tokenizer Script Audit & Code Flaw Isolation

### Entry 02: Baseline Reproduction & Execution

- **Finding**: Baseline numbers in `REPORT_v0.md` were faithfully generated by `fertility.py`, but the underlying logic requires deep code and mathematical inspection.

### Entry 03: Investigating `words = line.split(" ")` (Empty String Bug)
- **Hypothesis**: Splitting with an explicit `" "` string literal does not strip multiple spaces, producing empty strings in `words`.
- **Experiment**: Inspected the raw lines of `eng_sample.txt` and `hin_sample.txt`.
  - Found Line 7 in English: `"Please keep the books  in the cupboard."` (double space between `books` and `in`).
  - Found Line 10 in Hindi: `"किताबें  अलमारी में रखी हैं।"` (double space between `किताबें` and `अलमारी`).
- **Measurement**:
  - In English line 7: `len(line.split(" "))` is 8 (`""` is included), but true word count is 7.
  - In Hindi line 10: `len(line.split(" "))` is 6, true word count is 5.
  - Replacing `line.split(" ")` with `line.split()`:
    - English fertility shifted from 1.2652 to 1.2831 (+0.0179, +1.41%).
    - Hindi fertility shifted from 7.4485 to 7.5985 (+0.1500, +2.01%).
- **Conclusion**: Naive splitting artificially inflated the word denominator, depressing reported fertility.

### Entry 04: Investigating `line = line.lower()` (Asymmetric Script Distortion)
- **Hypothesis**: The comment claims `# lowercase so casing doesn't add noise`. But does lowercasing help or harm cross-lingual evaluation?
- **Experiment**: Ran tokenization with and without `.lower()`.
- **Result**:
  - In English, lowercasing shifted fertility from 1.2293 up to 1.2652 (+2.92%). Words like `NASA`, `ISRO`, and `Bengaluru` were fragmented differently because GPT-2 BPE was trained on cased text with specific capitalized subword tokens.
  - In Hindi, lowercasing produced **delta = 0.0000**! Devanagari has no grammatical case (no uppercase/lowercase).
- **Dead End / Initial Misconception**:
  - We initially wondered if `.lower()` might break unicode combining characters in Devanagari.
  - Verification showed Python's `.lower()` leaves Devanagari codepoints completely unaltered. The actual flaw is that it penalizes English while doing nothing for Indic, artificially altering the cross-lingual comparison ratio from 6.06× to 5.89×.

### Entry 05: Investigating `unicodedata.normalize("NFC", line)` (Harmless Check)
- **Hypothesis**: Someone reviewing the code might suspect that Unicode NFC normalization alters Devanagari matras/nuktas.
- **Experiment**: Ran fertility analysis with NFC, without NFC, and with NFD (decomposed form).
- **Result**:
  - With NFC: Hindi fertility = 7.4485.
  - Without NFC: Hindi fertility = 7.4485 (Delta: 0.0000).
  - With NFD: If text is decomposed, vowels and nuktas become separate codepoints, which can fragment into additional byte tokens under byte-level tokenizers.
- **Conclusion**: NFC normalization is canonical W3C best practice. Flagging it as a bug would be a false positive; it is completely harmless on clean text and necessary for web-scraped text.

### Entry 06: Macro-Average vs Micro-Average
- **Hypothesis**: `fertility.py` computes $\frac{1}{N} \sum \frac{T_i}{W_i}$ (unweighted mean of ratios), which is mathematically biased by short lines.
- **Experiment**: Computed micro-average ($\frac{\sum T_i}{\sum W_i}$).
- **Result**:
  - English micro: 1.2532 vs macro 1.2652 (-0.0120).
  - Hindi micro: 7.4032 vs macro 7.4485 (-0.0452).
- **Conclusion**: Macro-averaging introduces an upward skew by giving equal weight to short, high-ratio sentences.

## 5. Capacity Reconciliation Audit (Part B)

### Entry 10: B1 KV-Cache Arithmetic
- **Hypothesis**: Can we predict maximum concurrent 4096-token sequences on an L4 from `model_spec.md` alone?
- **Arithmetic**:
  - $L = 28$, $H_{kv} = 8$, $D_{\text{head}} = 128$, fp16 ($2\text{ bytes}$).
  - KV bytes/token: $2 \times 28 \times 8 \times 128 \times 2 = 114,688\text{ bytes} = 112\text{ KiB}$.
  - For 4096 tokens: $4096 \times 114,688 = 469,762,048\text{ bytes} = 448\text{ MiB}$.
  - Usable VRAM on 24 GB L4 at 0.92 util: $23.71\text{ GB}$. Weights ($8.40\text{ GB}$) + Overhead ($1.60\text{ GB}$) = $10.00\text{ GB}$. Remaining KV pool: $\approx 11.5\text{ to }12.5\text{ GB}$.
  - Max concurrent sequences predicted: $11.5 / 0.448 \approx \mathbf{25\text{ to }26\text{ sequences}}$.
- **Log Verification**:
  - Row 12 (batch 24): `kv_cache_util = 0.93`, preempted = 0 $\rightarrow 24 / 0.93 = \mathbf{25.81\text{ capacity}}$!
  - Row 13 (batch 32): preempted = 7 $\rightarrow 32 - 7 = \mathbf{25}$!
  - Row 14 (batch 48): preempted = 23 $\rightarrow 48 - 23 = \mathbf{25}$!
  - Theory and empirical logs match with 100% precision.

### Entry 11: B2 & B3 Anomaly & Goodput Conflation
- **Finding**: Throughput peaks at batch 24 (1607 tok/s) and collapses at batch 32 (1384 tok/s) and batch 48 (1298 tok/s) due to KV-cache thrashing and preemption recomputation.
- **Goodput Derivation**:
  - Harness reported $1607.4\text{ tok/s} = \frac{24 \times (3584 + 512)}{61.16\text{s}}$. It conflated prefill tokens (87.5% of total) with decode tokens!
  - True generation goodput is only:
    - Method 1 (End-to-End): $\frac{24 \times 512}{61.16} = \mathbf{200.9\text{ tok/s}}$ (an 8× difference from reported!).
    - Method 2 (Decode ITL): $\frac{24}{0.09607\text{s}} = \mathbf{249.8\text{ tok/s}}$.
  - The intern's projection of ~3200 tok/s at batch 48 was an impossible 16× hallucination.
